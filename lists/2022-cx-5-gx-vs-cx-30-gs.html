Verdict: start with the CX-30 GS

For a first car in Brampton on a G2, with low insurance, reliability, and value under $25,000 as the priorities, the CX-30 GS is the safer default. It's the better-equipped car, and it's the only one with a real insurance number: your existing TD quote of $6,378/yr. No CX-5 quote exists yet, and the claims evidence points to the CX-5 costing the same to modestly more — at ~$6.4k a year, even a 5% difference is ~$320/yr, which swamps the ~$110/yr fuel saving either way.

When the CX-5 GX wins: if you regularly carry adults in the back, haul bulky cargo, or want to tow (907 kg), the CX-5 gives you that space for no purchase premium — used, it asks the same or slightly less than the CX-30 GS despite costing $1,150 more new. The condition: get a VIN-specific TD quote for the actual car first, and only take the CX-5 if it lands within roughly $300/yr of the CX-30's $6,378.

One thing neither car makes you worry about: both Canadian 2022 trims have no cylinder deactivation — verified in Mazda Canada's own spec documents. It's the simplest, most proven version of Mazda's 2.5L, with none of the CD cracked-head or vibration baggage.

Which one fits how you'll actually use it?

CX-30 GS. The rear seat and cargo gaps won't touch your daily life, you get the better cabin equipment, the easier parking (10.6 m turning circle vs 11.9 m), and the insurance cost you already know: $6,378/yr quoted.

The $25,000 budget, honestly

Under $25,000 is realistic for either car — but in the GTA right now it buys a ~70,000–110,000 km example at the cash price, not a low-km cream puff. Watch for ads with a $2,000 finance credit baked in (cash price $2,000 higher), and remember Ontario dealer prices are before tax: a $24,950 car is roughly $28,200 out the door after 13% HST and licensing. If $25,000 is an all-in cap, your target asking price is about $22,100 — which in October 2026 means ~110,000+ km examples of either car.

Equipment: where the trims actually differ

This is a base trim (GX) against a mid trim (GS), and it shows — except for the screen, where the CX-5's base trim is the one that's better. Both cars run the same Mazda Connect system through a rotary Commander controller; neither screen is a driver touchscreen.

CX-30 GS only

  • Heated, leather-wrapped steering wheel (and leather-wrapped shift knob)
  • Dual-zone automatic climate control with rear console air vents — the CX-5 GX has manual single-zone A/C, rear air only via ducts under the front seats
  • 8-speaker audio (Mazda Harmonic Acoustics) vs the CX-5 GX's 4 speakers
  • Driver Attention Alert — in the CX-30 GS equipment list; not in the CX-5 GX walk
  • 18″ alloy wheels

Watch for the GS Luxury Package ($2,200 new, common used): leatherette seats, 10-way power driver's seat with lumbar and memory, power moonroof, auto-dimming rearview mirror, mirrors with reverse tilt-down and memory. Many "GS" ads describing a sunroof or leather are Luxury Package cars — verify by VIN and photos.

CX-5 GX only

  • 10.25″ centre display vs the CX-30's 8.8″ — same system, bigger screen
  • 40/20/40 split rear seat with manual recline and ski pass-through
  • 907 kg towing rating
  • Multi-link rear suspension; 17″ wheels with taller, cheaper tires
  • Three 12V outlets

Shared — both cars get all of this

  • i-Activ AWD; 6-speed automatic with Drive Selection; G-Vectoring Control Plus
  • Heated front seats (3-stage); LED headlights (auto on/off), LED DRLs and taillights; rain-sensing wipers; heated power mirrors with turn signals; rear privacy glass
  • Push-button start and fob keyless entry; electronic parking brake with auto-hold
  • Apple CarPlay / Android Auto; rearview camera; temporary spare tire; engine immobilizer
  • The full i-Activsense suite (details below) — between these two specific trims, the safety equipment is effectively equal
  • Mazda Connected Services 2-year trial — already expired on a 2022; a subscription is required to keep it

Neither car has: a power liftgate (both are manual), a power driver's seat (except CX-30 GS Luxury), a moonroof (except option packages), leather (both are cloth; CX-30 Luxury is leatherette), Bose audio, head-up display, 360° camera, parking sensors, wireless charging, heated rear seats, or SiriusXM. Don't let an ad imply otherwise.

Safety — a tie, with one CX-5 footnote

Both cars carry Mazda Radar Cruise Control with Stop & Go, Smart Brake Support (front), Smart City Brake Support with pedestrian detection, advanced blind-spot monitoring, rear cross-traffic alert, lane-departure warning with lane-keep assist, high-beam control, and distance/forward-obstruction warnings; the CX-30 GS adds Driver Attention Alert. Both are IIHS 2022 Top Safety Pick+ and NHTSA 5-star overall. The one distinction: the CX-5 was the first — and at the time the only one of 20 small SUVs — to earn a "Good" rating in IIHS's newer, tougher side-impact test.

Note the asymmetry inside the CX-30 range: a CX-30 GX gets only blind-spot/rear-cross-traffic/camera. It's the GS trim that brings the CX-30 up to the CX-5 GX's safety level — one more reason the GS, specifically, is the right CX-30 to buy.

Reliability, recalls & warranty

Both cars run the non-turbo, non-CD 2.5L with the proven 6-speed automatic — the lowest-risk Mazda powertrain combination of the era. No CVT, no turbo, no cylinder deactivation on either target trim.

CX-5 GX — the cleaner record

  • Consumer Reports' 2022 reliability survey: above-average predicted reliability (CX-30: average). Mazda ranked 4th as a brand
  • No 2022 recall found in the Transport Canada / NHTSA-based indexes searched — still run the VIN (one NHTSA-data aggregator lists the 2022 CX-5 at 0 recalls / 50 complaints; indicative only)
  • The known CX-5 horror stories — CD cracked heads, turbo oil consumption — do not apply to the GX's plain 2.5
  • Watch item: LED headlight fan noise or failure is expensive out of warranty — check both headlights carefully, listen for fan hum or grinding

CX-30 GS — one recall to verify

  • Recall 5823D (NHTSA 23V-275): certain 2022–2023 CX-30s had possible internal damage to the ABS hydraulic control unit — increased brake-pedal travel, reduced braking feel. ~2,556 units across the US and Canada; dealer inspection and replacement if needed, free. Only certain VINs — check the exact car
  • Consumer Reports: average predicted reliability; some owners reported engine-computer issues
  • Frequent "low battery risk" warnings / parasitic draw were addressed by a March 2022 BCM software update (TSB 09-010/22 family), plus infotainment updates — ask for proof they were applied
  • A 2022's original 12V battery is at end-of-life in 2026 regardless — budget for one

Warranty: what's actually left on a 2022, bought now

Mazda Canada's Mazda Unlimited terms are 3 yr / unlimited km comprehensive (with roadside assistance), 5 yr / unlimited km powertrain, 7 yr / unlimited km anti-perforation. A 2022's in-service date is typically Dec 2021 to fall 2022, so in October 2026:

  • Comprehensive: expired (ended 2024–2025)
  • Powertrain: months left at best — it expires Dec 2026 to ~fall 2027 depending on the car's exact in-service date; early-2022 registrations are down to ~2–4 months
  • Anti-perforation: runs to 2028–2029

Get the in-service date by VIN from a Mazda dealer — it, not the model year, decides what's left. Bought as Mazda Certified Pre-Owned, the car carries its remaining factory warranty — 5-year powertrain, unlimited km — plus a CPO top-up that runs only until the earlier of 6 months after the factory powertrain expires (or the sale date, whichever is later) or 10,000 km from the odometer at sale. For a 2022, that is roughly powertrain into 2027 depending on the in-service date, plus that top-up — not 7 years / 140,000 km. Dealer pages vary (some still advertise the old 7-year / 140,000 km figure, and the official site does not list an exchange privilege), so price any CPO premium on this shorter coverage and get the warranty certificate for the specific VIN in writing before relying on it.

Both cars, Ontario salt: inspect rear brake discs and calipers (a repeated CX-30 complaint), subframe and underbody corrosion, and AWD rear-differential service history.

Why the estimate points that way

  • Theft is a wash, and both are low-risk. Neither car appears on Équité Association's Top-10 most-stolen lists for Canada or Ontario in either the 2023-data or 2024-data lists — those are dominated by the CR-V, RAV4/Highlander, Lexus RX, Ram 1500, F-150 and Wrangler. Neither car should attract a high-theft surcharge.
  • Claims costs lean CX-5. Published IBC CLEAR data (via InsuranceHotline) for the 2020 CX-5 GS AWD — the latest year published there — shows relative claim-cost indexes of Collision 137, Comprehensive 99, DCPD 169 (100 = average): collision and Ontario repair-cost claims run materially above average. No equivalent published CX-30 table was found; the CX-30 is also the smaller, cheaper-to-replace car ($29,350 vs $30,500 new), which points the same direction.
  • Safety doesn't separate them — Top Safety Pick+ and 5-star for both.
  • Your profile dominates the vehicle. Ontario's 2026 median premium for all Mazdas and all drivers is ~$2,124/yr (MyChoice) versus your $6,378 — Brampton + G2 + new-driver status roughly triples the premium before vehicle choice is even considered. Postal code and licence history are the first-order levers; the car is second-order.
  • Check it yourself: IBC's How Cars Measure Up tool compares 2022 CX-5 vs 2022 CX-30 on real 2019–2024 claims data — run the two models there directly. Its model scores aren't published as a table, so they couldn't be pulled into this page.

Before you buy: checks for each car

Both cars

  • Run the exact VIN: Mazda dealer for in-service date, remaining powertrain warranty, and recall/TSB completion; CARFAX Canada for claims — Ontario DCPD claims may not all appear, so get a pre-purchase inspection regardless
  • Compare the cash / certified total plus HST, not the finance-price headline; confirm OMVIC all-in pricing and refuse add-on fees
  • Verify trim and package from the VIN/build, not the ad — CX-30 "GS with sunroof/leather" means the Luxury Package, and CX-5 ads frequently mislabel GX/GS
  • Pre-purchase inspection on a lift: underbody and salt corrosion, rear brakes, AWD operation, tires (matching set, date codes), 12V battery age
  • Confirm both keys, spare tire and tools are present, and that Mazda Connect and CarPlay work with software updates applied

CX-30 GS specifically

  • Recall 5823D (ABS hydraulic control unit) — confirm the inspection/repair was done for that VIN
  • Battery history: low-battery warnings or replacements; confirm the BCM software update was applied
  • Sit in the back seat and check rear visibility in person — the deal-breaker items for this car
  • Rear brake disc and caliper rust
  • A/C performance — an ambient-temperature sensor fault affected fully-auto-A/C Mazdas of this era, and the GS has auto climate

CX-5 GX specifically

  • Confirm it really is a 2022 GX with no cylinder deactivation — a 2023 GX does have CD; check the build/spec, not just the badge, if cross-shopping years
  • LED headlights: listen for fan hum or grinding and check both beams — replacement assemblies are expensive out of warranty
  • Power-liftgate complaints do not apply — the GX gate is manual, one less failure point
  • Test the rear-seat 40/20/40 releases and recline function
  • Check for water leaks around the liftgate and roof rails on inspection
