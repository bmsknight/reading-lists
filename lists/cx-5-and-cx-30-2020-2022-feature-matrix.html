CX-5 & CX-30, 2020–2022
The full feature matrix, trim by trim

Every Canadian trim of the CX-5 and CX-30 for 2020, 2021 (including the mid-year 2021.5 CX-5) and 2022, with features as rows and trims as columns — built only from Mazda Canada's own “Specifications & Features” decks and pricing releases. US trim names (Sport, Touring, Preferred, Carbon Edition) never existed in Canada and aren't used here. Cylinder deactivation gets its own highlighted row in every table, because it's the easiest thing to get wrong.

Cylinder deactivation

CX-5 GX: never. Every non-turbo CX-5 above GX (GS, Kuro, GT, Sport Design) has CD in all years. CX-30: CD on the 2020 GT only — dropped entirely for 2021 and 2022.

All-wheel drive

Optional on GX/GS and standard higher up for 2020–2021. For 2022, AWD became standard on every trim of both models in Canada — FWD was dropped.

Screens

CX-5: 7″ in 2020 (8″ only on GT Turbo / Signature) → 8″ on every trim for 2021 → 10.25″ at 2021.5 with the new Mazda Connect. CX-30: 8.8″ in all three years — and it's never a touchscreen.

Base-trim safety split

CX-30 GX (all years) and CX-5 GX (2020–2021) get no adaptive cruise, no Smart Brake Support, no lane-keep — those start at GS. CX-5 GX only caught up at 2021.5.

Year over year: added, removed, changed

What actually moved between model years — including features that slid down to cheaper trims and the ones that quietly disappeared.

Mazda CX-5

2020 → 2021

  • AddedKuro Edition — a GS-AWD-with-Comfort-Package base plus Garnet Red leather, black 19″ wheels, 10-way power/memory driver seat.
  • Added100th Anniversary Edition (Signature-based, Snowflake White Pearl / Garnet Red Nappa, one year only).
  • Moved ↓8″ screen to every trim — in 2020 only GT Turbo and Signature had it; GT non-turbo and below had 7″.
  • Moved ↓Off-Road Traction Assist became available on GX/GS with AWD (was GT Turbo / Signature only).
  • AddedSignature gains Smart City Brake Support – Rear and Driver Attention Alert; GT gains wireless Apple CarPlay.
  • ChangedAWD option price fell $2,000 → $1,500 on GX/GS. Engines and CD pattern unchanged.
  • RemovedColours Titanium Flash Mica (all) and Eternal Blue (GX).

2021 → 2021.5 (mid-year)

  • Added10.25″ display + latest-generation Mazda Connect, standard on all trims — replaces the 8″ touchscreen system.
  • AddedMazda Connected Services (remote start/stop, lock/unlock, health report via MyMazda app), available from Fall 2021 with a free trial.
  • AddedGX gains the full i-Activsense suite: MRCC with Stop & Go, Smart Brake Support Front, Pedestrian Detection, Lane-Keep Assist, Lane Departure Warning, High Beam Control.
  • Added19″ wheels newly available on GS with AWD; Kuro gains a black leather with red stitching interior option alongside Garnet Red.
  • RemovedWireless Apple CarPlay on GT — dropped with the move to the new Mazda Connect (wired CarPlay/Android Auto remain).
  • ChangedPrices up roughly $350–$550 per trim. CD, engines and FWD availability unchanged. 100th Anniversary absent from the 2021.5 price table (presumed discontinued — unverified).

2021.5 → 2022 (facelift)

  • AddedAWD standard on every trim; Sport Design trim added; Mi-Drive (Normal/Sport/Off-Road) on Kuro and up; Traffic Jam Assist on Sport Design and Signature; Qi wireless charging on GT.
  • RemovedFWD entirely; separate LED fog lamps (fog function integrated into the new headlights).
  • ChangedFacelift: new 3D-texture grille and signature wing, new bumpers and tailgate, larger tailpipes, rectangular LED lamps; redesigned seats, revised suspension damping and frame rigidity, more responsive 6AT. Signature gets body-colour cladding.
  • ChangedGS Comfort Package now bundles 19″ wheels and a reversible trunk board — price $1,500 → $2,100. Turbo power 250 → 256 hp (93 octane). Cargo dips slightly to 871 / 1,680 L.
  • AddedA −$400 GT “standard sound, without SiriusXM” delete variant (6 speakers, no Bose/SiriusXM) appears in the deck. CD pattern unchanged.

Mazda CX-30

2020 → 2021

  • RemovedCylinder deactivation (GT) — the only CD CX-30 disappears after one year.
  • AddedGT Turbo option (+$2,400): black-finish 18″ wheels, Jet Black Mica mirrors, larger exhaust, Turbo badges.
  • Moved ↓Auto on/off headlights and rain-sensing wipers to GX (2020 GX had auto-off-only headlights and no rain-sensing wipers).
  • ChangedAWD option $2,000 → $1,500. GS Luxury Package gains mirror reverse-tilt / memory-seat link in the deck description; Luxury stitch colour changes from warm silver to dark grey for November production.
  • ChangedNo changes to screen, audio, or the GX-vs-GS safety split.

2021 → 2022

  • RemovedFWD entirely.
  • AddedAWD and Off-Road Traction Assist standard on all trims; Mazda Connected Services standard (2-year trial).
  • AddedGT Advanced Visibility Package ($800): 360° View Monitor, front parking sensors, Traffic Jam Assist — the CX-30's first 360° camera. The GT Turbo now requires this package.
  • ChangedGS Luxury Package $1,900 → $2,200. Colours: Platinum Quartz Metallic added (GS/GT), Garnet Red leather becomes a GT interior option, Titanium Flash Mica dropped.
  • AddedA −$400 GT “Mazda Harmonic Acoustics, without SiriusXM” delete variant (8 speakers instead of Bose 12). CD remains absent on every trim.

Gotchas for a used buyer

Where listings and trim badges mislead — all traceable to the decks above.

  1. CD traps run in both directions. Avoiding CD? Any CX-5 GS/Kuro/GT non-turbo, any year, has it — only GX and turbos don't. Expecting CD fuel savings from a 2020 CX-30 GT review? 2021–2022 CX-30s have none at all, and the 2020 GS never had it — only the 2020 GT.
  2. “2021 CX-5” is two different cars. Early 2021: 8″ touchscreen, old Mazda Connect, GX without adaptive cruise. 2021.5: 10.25″ wide display, new Mazda Connect, Connected Services, full safety suite on GX — but GT loses wireless CarPlay. The screen is the visual tell: 8″ touch vs 10.25″ wide.
  3. Screens are easy to confuse in photos. A 2020 CX-5 GT non-turbo has the 7″ screen while the same-year GT Turbo/Signature have 8″ — the GT badge alone doesn't tell you. The CX-30's 8.8″ is never a touchscreen in any year; the CX-5's 7″/8″ (2020–2021) are touchscreens and the 10.25″ (2021.5+) is the commander-knob system.
  4. Package features get advertised as trim features. CX-5 GS: moonroof, dual-zone climate and rear console vents exist only with the Comfort Package (which itself requires AWD) — a GS FWD or non-Comfort GS AWD has manual climate and no moonroof, though it keeps the power liftgate and heated steering wheel. CX-30 GS: leatherette, power/memory driver seat and moonroof exist only with the Luxury Package; a plain GS is cloth with an 8-way manual seat. In the decks' price tables the CX-30 Luxury Package is listed only in combination with AWD.
  5. Safety mislabelling is routine. CX-30 GX (all years) and CX-5 GX (2020/2021) are advertised with “adaptive cruise / lane keep” — they don't have MRCC, Smart Brake Support, lane-keep or High Beam Control. They have Blind Spot Monitoring, Rear Cross-Traffic and a rear camera (CX-5 GX adds Smart City Brake Support Front). Those features start at GS — or at 2021.5 for the CX-5 GX.
  6. 2022 delete-option cars. Some 2022 GTs were built with the −$400 “without SiriusXM” package: 6 speakers (CX-5) or 8-speaker Harmonic (CX-30) instead of Bose 10/12, and no SiriusXM. A “2022 GT” ad claiming Bose needs the actual build verified.
  7. Turbo ≠ top-trim content. A CX-30 GT Turbo is a GT plus an engine option (2022: plus the mandatory Advanced Visibility Package); a 2020/2021 CX-5 GT Turbo doesn't gain Signature items (360° camera, parking sensors, SCBS-Rear, Driver Attention Alert). And a 2022 CX-5 Sport Design non-turbo still has CD and no paddle shifters.
  8. Kuro ≠ GT. The CX-5 Kuro is a GS+Comfort-based style trim — no Bose, no head-up display, no navigation, no ventilated or heated rear seats, despite the leather and 19″ wheels. In 2022 it gains Mi-Drive and the 7″ LCD meter set, still without GT audio/safety extras.
  9. Small-item splits on the CX-30 GX. GX mirrors are non-heated in all three years (heated starts at GS), and the 2020 GX lacks the rain-sensing wipers and auto-on headlights that 2021+ GX has. The CX-5 GX, by contrast, has heated mirrors and rain-sensing wipers in every year.
  10. Things no trim has. No CX-30 offers heated rear seats or ventilated seats in any year, and no 2020–2021 CX-30 has a 360° camera. Neither model offers roof rails (the CX-5 has roof-rack mouldings only), a rear-seat alert is not listed in any deck, and front parking sensors exist only on CX-5 Signature / CX-30 with the 2022 Advanced Visibility Package.
  11. MSRPs drifted within model years. Mazda Canada raised prices mid-year — e.g. 2022 CX-5 GX was $30,200 at launch and $30,500 in the May 2022 deck; 2021 CX-5 GX went $28,500 → $28,600. An ad quoting “original MSRP” may legitimately match either figure. All MSRPs here exclude $1,950 freight & PDE and the $100 A/C surcharge.

What couldn't be verified

Stated plainly rather than filled in from US data or adjacent years.

  • No 2021.5 CX-5 Specifications & Features PDF exists on Mazda Canada's media filecache. The 2021.5 panel rests on Mazda Canada's own May 12, 2021 mid-year update release. Anything that release doesn't name — DRSS/Forward Obstruction Warning on the 2021.5 GX, the exact packaging of the GS 19″ wheels, 100th Anniversary continuation, 2021.5 cargo volume — is marked Unverified.
  • 2022 CX-5 Advanced Keyless Entry conflict: the Nov 30, 2021 pricing release lists it among GS additions; the spec deck's Equipment Walk places it only in the GS Comfort Package (as in 2020–2021). The deck is treated as authoritative on this page — verify on the actual car.
  • 2020 CX-30 Luxury Package mirror functions: the 2020 deck's Luxury description omits mirror reverse-tilt / memory link (they're listed as GT items); the 2021 deck adds them to the Luxury description. Check individual 2020 Luxury cars.
  • Wireless CarPlay on 2021 Signature / 100th Anniversary is by inheritance — the deck lists it under GT and Signature “includes GT” equipment — rather than a separate line item.
  • Rear seat alert / rear occupant reminder: not listed in any of the six decks, for any trim or year.
