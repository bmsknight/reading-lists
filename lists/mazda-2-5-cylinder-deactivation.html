Cylinder deactivation in Mazda's 2.5L SkyActiv-G: a tiny fuel saving with one expensive catch

Deep research on the 2.5-litre naturally aspirated SkyActiv-G (engine family PY) with cylinder deactivation — how it works, the 2019 rocker-arm recall, the cracked-cylinder-head problem, which model years are safer buys, and what Mazda actually changed. Built from Mazda, NHTSA and Transport Canada documents first, owner forums second — and labelled so you can tell which is which.

2.5L NA SkyActiv-G (PY) CX-5 · CX-30 · Mazda3 · Mazda6 North America, 2018–2025 Not the 2.5T turbo — a different engine

The verdict, in plain language

1

The idea is sound; the payoff is tiny. CD shuts off the two outside cylinders at light cruise. Mazda's best-case claim is ~20% better economy at a steady 40 km/h, but in official ratings the real difference is usually 0–1 mpg — roughly 3%.

2

Two separate problems — don't merge them. A 2019 software recall (rocker arms) is solved with a free reprogram. The cracked cylinder heads on CD engines are a hardware issue Mazda has documented but never recalled, with no warranty extension for the NA engine.

3

The safest used buy never had CD. In the 2020–2024 window those cars exist: Canadian CX-5 GX (2020–2022), Canadian CX-30 (2021–2022), 2.0L Mazda3s, and some late 2024 CX-5s built without CD.

4

The VIN tells the truth. Trim badges and dealer listings are unreliable. 8th VIN digit M = with cylinder deactivation, L = without. Check a VIN below.

How it works

At light, steady cruise the engine shuts down its two outside cylinders — No. 1 and No. 4 — and runs on the middle two. (Some low-quality web guides claim cylinders 2 and 3 are deactivated; every Mazda source says 1 and 4.)

  • The switch

    Cylinders 1 and 4 carry switchable hydraulic lash adjusters (SHLAs). Under PCM control, an oil control valve changes oil pressure and releases a lock pin inside the adjuster; the rocker arm's pivot point collapses, so both intake and exhaust valves stay closed. Mazda

  • Trapped air spring

    With the valves shut, fuel and spark to 1 and 4 stop. The air trapped inside compresses and expands like a spring, and with only two cylinders pumping, pumping losses and mechanical resistance fall. Mazda

  • Smoothing it out

    A centrifugal pendulum in the torque converter of the 6-speed automatic counterbalances the vibration of two-cylinder running, and airflow, fuel injection and ignition timing are actively managed through every switch. Mazda

  • No off switch

    The PCM decides continuously which mode is better "regardless of the driver's operations." There is no driver control to disable it. Mazda owner's manual

  • Oil matters

    Spec is 0W-20 — and it matters more than usual, because the lash adjusters and the CD oil control valve are themselves oil-operated. Debris in the oil path can disable the system (fault code P3400 — see other issues).

When it engages

  • Light load, steady cruise — Mazda's design sweet spot is a steady 40–80 km/h (25–50 mph); owners also see it at higher steady highway speeds when load is low enough
  • It drops out the moment torque demand rises — all four cylinders return, in Mazda's words, "instantaneously"

When it won't run

  • Selector in P, N or R (or 1st gear on manual cars)
  • Engine oil or coolant still cold
  • Immediately after a battery disconnect

Pros and cons

Every item is tagged by evidence: Documented means a Mazda, NHTSA or Transport Canada document or official rating backs it; Owner opinion is what drivers report; Speculation is claimed online but proven nowhere.

Pros

  • Real fuel saving at steady cruise Best at 40–80 km/h; ~3% in official ratings, up to Mazda's claimed ~20% at a steady 40 km/h. Documented
  • No power penalty Ask for power and all four cylinders return immediately — full 186–191 hp is always available. Documented
  • Usually imperceptible When it works as designed, most owners never feel the switch at all; plenty of owners report they only know it exists from the dashboard monitor. Owner opinion
  • Kept the simple engine viable CD helped Mazda meet economy and emissions targets without downsizing, turbocharging or a CVT. On the 2023 Mazda3, an "improved cylinder deactivation system" arrived alongside a rise to 191 hp with better economy. Documented
  • Nothing beyond that Honestly: no evidence was found that CD extends engine life, reduces wear, or helps resale. If anything, known problem years hurt resale. Finding

Cons

  • Cracked cylinder head / oil leak The single biggest financial risk — specific to the CD version of the engine. US$5,000–7,500 dealer repair quotes, no recall, no warranty extension. Detailed below. Documented
  • Rocker-arm / stall risk (early software) The original PCM software could let an intake rocker arm fall out of place on the switch back to four cylinders. Recalled in 2019 and fixed by reprogram. Documented
  • Vibration at ~1,500–1,650 rpm A low-frequency drone/buzz in two-cylinder mode that Mazda itself issued a fix for (TSB 01-009/20) while calling the underlying vibration "normal vehicle behavior while cylinder deactivation is active." Documented
  • Low-speed surging Roughly 2 cycles per second in locked-up 1st gear on 2018–2020 CD cars; fixed by a PCM update (TSB 05-002/20). Documented
  • Ticking from the switchable lash adjusters Air bubbles in the SHLA oil passage cause idle tapping; Mazda modified the part for increased oil supply (TSB 01-002/21). Documented
  • Complexity everywhere A different head casting, an extra oil control valve, a different torque converter — and on 2023 CD Mazda3/CX-30, even a different transmission fluid (ATF-A7, not interchangeable with ATF-FZ). No way to switch any of it off. Documented
  • Thermal cycling / uneven wear / carbon claims Often asserted in forums as the cause of the head cracks. No Mazda document states any of these, and carbon build-up is a generic direct-injection issue on all SkyActiv-G engines, CD or not. Speculation

The reliability concerns, investigated separately

The internet tends to mash these into one "cylinder deactivation problem." They are distinct issues with distinct statuses — one closed by recall, one still open, and a tail of smaller documented faults.

1 · The 2019 recall — PCM software could drop a rocker arm

  • Affected: 2018–2019 CX-5, 2018–2019 Mazda6, 2019 Mazda3 — production roughly October 2017 to May 2019, depending on model. Some cars left the factory with the improved software and were never recalled.
  • Root cause, in Mazda's words:

"The software controlling the hydraulic valve clearance adjuster may operate improperly when transitioning from cylinder deactivation to full cylinder activation modes. As a result, an intake valve rocker arm may come out of position and make contact with internal engine parts, which may cause an engine misfire, loss of engine power, and/or MIL illumination" — worst case, "loss of engine power or engine stall without the ability to be restarted."

  • Fix: a free dealer PCM reprogram with improved hydraulic valve adjustment control software. Remedy available from August 2019. Mazda reported no accidents, injuries or deaths from the defect.
  • For a buyer: this is a closed issue if the reprogram was done — and it's checkable by VIN through Mazda Canada, Transport Canada or a dealer's system. An already-fixed 2019 is not, on this ground alone, a car to avoid. The head issue below is the live one.

Sources: Mazda USA recall statement · NHTSA 19V-497 dealer notice (PDF) · CarComplaints defect-report summary

2 · Cracked cylinder head — oil leak on the CD engine

No recall · No warranty extension

The cylinder head on the CD version of the 2.5 NA can crack at the rear of the head, above and around the exhaust manifold (some dealer reports describe leaks between cylinders 2 and 3 at the back of the head). An oil gallery runs there, so this engine leaks pressurised engine oil onto hot exhaust components — not coolant. That distinction matters; see the turbo comparison below.

  • Symptoms, consistent across dozens of NHTSA complaints: burning-oil smell in the cabin, smoke from under the hood, oil pooling or oil on the undertray, a falling oil level, eventually a low-oil warning — frequently with no check-engine light and no overheating warning at all.
  • Reported failure mileages are often low: complaints at roughly 26,000, 32,000, 35,000, 36,000–38,000, 44,300, ~47,000, 55,000 and 62,317 miles.
  • Out-of-warranty cost (US owner-reported quotes; Canadian prices will differ): $4,589.76 · ~$4,500–$6,000 · $5,100 · ~$5,500 independent vs. ~$7,500 dealer · $6,700 + tax · $6,788.51 · ~$7,000 · one $10,000 quote with no written diagnosis. Plan on US$5,000–7,500 at a dealer.
  • Warranty reality: in Canada the standard powertrain warranty is 5 years / unlimited km, so coverage turns on the car's in-service date, not mileage — as of October 2026 a 2020 is out, a 2021 is out or expiring, a 2023–2024 may still be covered. Owners just outside warranty repeatedly report Mazda denying or only partially goodwilling repairs.

What Mazda actually issued — service alerts, not a diagnosis

There is no NA "cracked head" TSB stating a root cause in the public NHTSA record. What Mazda issued is a chain of service alerts establishing a replacement cylinder-head assembly (part family PYY4-10-SJ0, with valves, springs, seats and cotters pre-installed) as a service part for the CD engine:

SA-058/2024 Jul 2020→ SA-031/216 May 2021→ SA-013/2216 Mar 2022→ SA-037/236 Jun 2023

The latest version applies to 2018–2023 CX-5, 2018–2021 Mazda6, 2019–2023 Mazda3 and 2020–2023 CX-30 (US spec) — all 2.5 with CD — and its own search keywords include "Engine Oil Leak, Oil Smell, Coolant leak, Warp head, Misfire, Low Compression, Burnt Valves, Rocker Arm." Dealers do perform head replacements under these alerts; owner invoices citing SA-013/22 and SA-031/21 are documented.

Do not let anyone tell you CSP11 covers this car. The widely cited 10-year / 120,000-mile warranty extension is for the 2.5T turbo's coolant leak only. NA owners who cite it are routinely denied — several NHTSA complaints make exactly that mistaken citation before reporting a denial.

Did cylinder deactivation cause the cracks?

Correlated — causation unproven

Honest answer: the cracks are correlated with CD by design, but Mazda has never stated a root cause for the NA crack, and no primary document proves that switching cylinders off causes it.

  • What is solid: only CD engines have this head. The CD head is a different casting, changed to package the switchable lash adjusters and their oil passages. Non-CD 2.5s don't appear in the service-alert scope and don't generate this complaint pattern. So at minimum, "cars with the CD head casting crack."
  • What is solid: one reported crack location — between cylinders 2 and 3 — is not at a deactivated cylinder, which sits awkwardly with the popular "deactivated cylinders cool down and thermally cycle the head" theory.
  • What is solid: for the turbo head (a different casting again), Mazda's own stated causes are production residual stress plus exhaust-system forces — a casting/stress story, not an operating-mode story. Suspecting something similar on the NA CD head is reasonable, but it is still an inference.
  • Claimed, not proven: the Mazdas247 technical community, citing a teardown video, says the wall at the crack site is only ~2–3 mm thick where the head was modified for CD — plausible and specific, but sourced to a video and forum measurement, not Mazda. Owner source
  • Claimed, not proven: that the 2019 recall's PCM change raised oil pressure and thereby stressed the head. No Mazda document links them. Speculation
  • Claimed, not proven: that a "revised design" head fixed production by 2021 and complaints "dropped sharply" (SlashGear). But the service alerts kept expanding through 2023 model years, complaints include a 2022 CX-5 and a 2023 CX-5 at ~35,000 miles, and owners who compared part numbers argue the service head shows no evident design change. Do not buy a 2022–2023 CD car assuming the head was "fixed in 2021."

Failure rate: unknown. Mazda has published no rate, forums attract failures, and hundreds of thousands of CD cars are driving without incident. The risk is best described as low-probability, high-cost, and uninsured by any Mazda program once the factory powertrain warranty expires — precisely the risk profile a used buyer should price in rather than panic about or dismiss.

3 · The rest of the CD-specific fault list

Documented, mostly TSB'd
  • Switchable-HLA tapping at idle — air bubbles in the SHLA oil passage. TSB 01-006/20, superseded by 01-002/21: Mazda modified the switchable HLA for increased oil supply. First step was a bleed procedure (hold 2,000 rpm for 5 minutes, twice); the updated bulletin replaces any adjuster that can be pressed down by finger. Applies to 2019–2021 Mazda3, 2018–2021 Mazda6 and CX-5, 2020–2021 CX-30. A related alert covers tapping on new/low-use cars after repeated short cold starts — presented by Mazda as an operating condition, not damage.
  • Vibration at 1,500–1,650 rpm — TSB 01-009/20 fits revised radiator mount brackets plus PCM software on the Mazda3, while noting the underlying two-cylinder vibration is normal behaviour.
  • Low-speed surging — TSB 05-002/20 PCM fix for 2018–2020 CD cars surging ~2×/second in locked-up 1st gear.
  • Torque-converter lock-up wear / 3–4 shift chirp — TSB 05-005/23 covers CD models with the 6-speed automatic only (2017–2024 CX-5, 2019–2024 Mazda3, 2020–2024 CX-30, 2018–2021 Mazda6): iron powder in the ATF from excessive lock-up clutch wear; remedy is converter replacement plus ATF renewal under powertrain warranty. Whether CD causes the wear is forum inference — CD cars use a different converter design, and the TSB itself only states the wear mechanism.
  • Front timing-cover oil seep — reseal TSB 01-006/23 (updated June 2024), again scoped to CD cars, with production cut-offs running to September 2023 builds (CX-5). Far cheaper than a head — and a common misdiagnosis in both directions, which is why a buyer needs any leak located, not just noted as "oil leak."
  • P3400 fault from a service error — plastic from an oil-filter wrapper can plug the CD oil control valve and light the check-engine lamp (SA-061/20 → SA-047/21). The system is sensitive to oil-path contamination; the valve usually isn't worn out.
  • Camshaft damage after HLA problems — individual forum reports exist, but there is no TSB and no pattern in the NHTSA record. Reported, not established. Owner reports
  • Oil consumption / carbon on the NA engine — no NA-CD-specific TSB, program or established pattern found. Check first whether "consumption" is actually the external head leak above.

Sources: TSB 01-002/21 — switchable HLA (PDF) · TSB 01-009/20 — vibration · SA-061/20 — P3400 · Front-cover reseal TSB (PDF)

What Mazda changed — and what it didn't

The pattern: the software problem got a real fix, the drivability complaints got TSBs, the valvetrain got a revised part — and the cracked head got a replacement part number and, in several markets, quiet deletion of the feature. No recall, no warranty extension, and no published root cause for the NA head, as of October 29, 2026.

  • Jul 2019

    Recall 3719F — PCM reprogram. Improved hydraulic valve-adjustment control software so a rocker arm can't be displaced on the switch back to four cylinders. Free, and it closes that specific risk. NHTSA 19V-497 · TC 2019-322

  • 2020

    Drivability TSBs. Radiator mount brackets + PCM software for the 1,500–1,650 rpm two-cylinder vibration (01-009/20); PCM update for low-speed surging (05-002/20).

  • Jul 2020 →

    A replacement head exists — as a service part. SA-058/20 first establishes a new cylinder-head assembly for the CD engine; the alert chain runs through SA-037/23 (Jun 2023) covering cars to the 2023 model year. Whether that head is a genuinely strengthened redesign is unverified — owners comparing part numbers report no evident design change.

  • Jan 2021

    Revised switchable lash adjusters. TSB 01-002/21: the SHLA is modified for increased oil supply to stop air-bubble tapping at idle.

  • 2021–2022

    Deletion as a fix — Canada drops CD on the CX-30. Canadian CX-30s lose the CD engine for 2021 and stay without it for 2022 (the US car keeps it). The Canadian CX-5 GX had already been non-CD since 2020 and stays that way through 2022.

  • 2023

    "Improved cylinder deactivation" on the Mazda3. Mazda pairs an improved CD system with a power rise to 191 hp and better economy ratings — while, in the same year, CD becomes standard on every NA CX-5 trim in Canada and the US. TSB 05-005/23 also adds a torque-converter replacement path for CD automatics with lock-up wear.

  • Late 2023

    CD disappears from many 2024 CX-5s — officially a parts shortage. From October 2023 production (Select/Preferred) and November 2023 (Carbon/Premium/Premium Plus), CD and i-Stop were dropped "due to supply shortage… will be reinstated once the supply recovers," per Mazda USA's own spec deck. Canadian advertising showed 2024 GX/GS without CD/i-Stop, Kuro/GT with. Result: 2024 is a mixed year, VIN by VIN.

  • Never

    No changed oil spec (0W-20 throughout), no NA head recall, no NA warranty extension, and no stated root cause for the NA crack. The 10-year/120,000-mile CSP11 extension belongs to the turbo engine only.

Buying a used 2020–2024 CX-30, CX-5 or Mazda3 in Ontario

The practical checklist that falls out of everything above.

  • Decode the VIN before anything else. 8th digit M = CD, L = no CD. Prefer an L car where one exists: Canadian CX-5 GX 2020–2022, Canadian CX-30 2021–2022, a 2.0L Mazda3, or a verified non-CD late-build 2024 CX-5.

  • On 2018–2019 cars, get recall 3719F completion in writing — Mazda Canada, Transport Canada or dealer printout by VIN. No proof, no deal.

  • Pre-purchase inspection with two specific targets: the rear of the cylinder head above the exhaust manifold (crack / oil) and the front timing cover (seep). "Oil leak" without a location is not an inspection result — the two leaks differ by thousands of dollars.

  • Check the oil yourself: level on the dipstick, and any burning-oil smell after a run. The head leak often sets no warning light — ask the seller directly whether the car uses oil between changes, and whether the head has ever been replaced (look for SA-013/22 or SA-031/21 on invoices).

  • Test-drive the CD operating zone: hold 1,500–1,650 rpm and a steady 80–100 km/h and feel for low-frequency vibration; listen for a chirp on the 3–4 upshift; listen for tapping at idle. Open the Fuel Efficiency Monitor and watch it switch 4↔2.

  • Do the warranty maths by in-service date, not model year. Canada's powertrain warranty is 5 years / unlimited km. In October 2026 most 2020–2021 cars are out; a 2023–2024 may still be in. An in-warranty CD car is a very different risk from an identical out-of-warranty one.

  • Reject the CSP11 sales pitch. If a seller says the head is "covered by Mazda's 10-year extension," that extension is turbo-only. On a non-turbo car it's either ignorance or a bluff — either way it reprices the car.

  • Price the risk on out-of-warranty CD cars. Budget or negotiate as if a head replacement (US$5,000–7,500 at a dealer; Canadian quotes will differ) is a real possibility — low-probability, but yours alone if it happens. Prefer 2022–2024 if you do buy CD, and treat any claim that the head was "fixed in 2021" as unproven.

What's still unknown — stated plainly

  • The true failure rate of NA CD cylinder heads. Mazda has published no rate, and no source in this research establishes one. Anyone quoting a percentage is guessing.
  • Whether the SA-037/23 replacement head is a strengthened redesign, and whether any production change improved the head mid-run. A "revised by 2021" claim circulates in secondary coverage; the alert chain expanding to 2023 models and documented 2022/2023 failures argue against treating it as settled.
  • Exact US trim-by-trim CD fitment for 2021–2022. Mazda's own published tables are inconsistent — which is why the VIN check above, not any table, is the authority.
  • Confidence overall: high on the recall, the service-alert chain, the turbo-only CSP11 distinction, the VIN code and the Canadian trim facts (all from primary documents); medium on US 2021–2022 trim fitment; low on failure rates and the redesign question.
